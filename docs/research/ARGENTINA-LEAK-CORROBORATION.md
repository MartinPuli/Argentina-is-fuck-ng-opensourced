## Counting and design implications

These are our analytical recommendations, not additional incident findings:

1. Keep separate totals for acknowledged disclosure, acknowledged intrusion/defacement, disputed allegation, enforcement update and historical recirculation. Report a denominator and date window beside every count.
2. Store the claimed value, unit and claimant separately from a verified value. Leave unique people and extraction dates unknown when evidence cannot establish them.
3. Group sources by their original evidence chain. Ten articles quoting one post do not create ten confirmations.
4. Link institutional and supplier identities without assuming their systems were all breached. Mark overlap as confirmed, suspected or unknown; do not equate shared field names with identical datasets.
5. A sponsor-facing demonstration can use synthetic records to show a public-document release gate, separate supplier permissions and an evidence-backed alert ledger. These cases motivate the scenarios; they do not prove our product would have prevented them.

No leaked samples were acquired, no marketplaces were visited, no systems were scanned, and no affected-party contact was made. Outstanding leads are not proof of either compromise or absence of compromise. Calendar-year CERT statistics belong in a separate aggregate series and cannot be added to this rolling, case-based ledger.
